About this case study and sources

About this case study. 10 users were drawn at random from everyone who started using Social Limits before August 2025, is still active, and has at least 45 days of step data in late summer of both years. We compare July to September 2025 with July to September 2026, the most recent three complete months, so seasons don't skew the comparison. One user has no step data for August 2026, so that month averages 9 users. With only 10 users, results are indicative and vary between people. All data is anonymised.

Unlock times. The time social media unlocked is when the day's step goal was met, in each user's local time, on days the goal was met (523 days in 2025, 680 in 2026). "Typical" is the median. Days the goal wasn't met, when social media stayed locked, aren't included.

How we estimate productive hours. The 2,607 extra steps a day equal about 26 minutes of brisk walking at roughly 100 steps a minute,2 or 182 minutes a week, above the 150 minutes the WHO recommends.3 RAND found that physically active workers lose 3.0 to 4.6 fewer working days a year to sick leave and reduced performance than inactive workers.1 At 7.5 hours a day that is 23 to 34 hours. It applies in full to an employee who moves from inactive to active, and it is an estimate from published research, not a measurement of these users' work.

  1. Hafner M. et al., The economic benefits of a more physically active population: An international analysis, RAND Corporation RR-4291, 2019. rand.org/t/RR4291
  2. Marshall S.J. et al., "Translating physical activity recommendations into a pedometer-based step goal: 3000 steps in 30 minutes", American Journal of Preventive Medicine 36(5), 2009.
  3. World Health Organization, Guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour, 2020.
